Script & leaf options

Sara's note on the first pass: the small script line above each section works, but the font reads a little casual and the leaves read as line drawings. Her two examples on the right show the brand's real leaf: soft watercolor silver-dollar eucalyptus in grey-green, spilling in from a corner, paired with a thin slanted handwriting. Every row below uses watercolor eucalyptus painted to match those examples, so the leaf question becomes how much and where, and the font question is the one left to decide.

Round 2, after the Nov 12 call. The leaves are approved: the watercolor eucalyptus stays, placed the way rows A and E place it, sparse, with no footer garland. That leaves the font. None of A to E landed outright, so rows F to J below are five more, each shown at desktop size and at the 22px it runs on a phone, where 70 to 80 percent of visitors will see it. A and E stay on the sheet as the fallbacks; C is retired.

Headlines stay readable

Every H1 and H2 stays in Instrument Sans caps as live text. Those lines are what Google reads to rank each page and what visitors skim first. No script, ever, at headline size.

Script is the small line only

The script appears once per section, on the line above the headline, at the size shown here. Below that size thin scripts stop being legible, which is the one thing worth keeping from Caveat.

Leaves are decoration

Images placed in a corner or along the footer, never behind text and never on photos or case-study pages. At most two per page. They add warmth without touching readability or search.

How to use this

  1. Pick a font from F to J, or fall back to A or E. The leaves are decided; every row shows the approved placement.
  2. Stock art welcome. If Sara has the original brochure artwork or a stock set she prefers, it drops straight into these placements. Best as vector (SVG, AI, EPS) or transparent PNG, licensed for web use.
  3. Timing. A pick this week goes into the Webflow build. If nothing here lands, send the font you like and we will check whether it is licensed for web; buying one is fine.

What does not change

  1. Headlines stay in Instrument Sans caps as live text, for readers and for search. The script is an accent above them, never a second headline face.
  2. Mono tags stay for before/after labels, card types and badges.
  3. Colors stay inside the current palette. The watercolor greens sit in the existing sage range; the mint band in D is the only new color on the sheet.
